## Research question

How do relations between humans, wild boar, and other ecological actors emerge and get
negotiated in Peweñantu when an invasive species becomes a contact zone between distinct
worlds — colonial, Indigenous, scientific, and more-than-human?

## Territory

Peweñantu is a transitional landscape in the Andean Araucanía, where native forests,
tree plantations, Mapuche communities, and hunting and management practices coexist under
ecological and political tension. Wild boar moves through these territories as an
unexpected actor: predator, food, pest, symbol.

## Approach

Drawing on Science and Technology Studies (STS), environmental humanities, and
multi-sited ethnography, the project combines fieldwork, interviews, and analysis of
public controversies around invasive species management.

## Team and institutions

The project is led by Manuel Tironi (UC) together with Nicolás Gálvez, Pelayo Benavides,
and the Winkulmapu Indigenous Association, with support from UC Sociology Institute, CEDEL Villarrica, and IDS.
